# Exoplanet Life Study Framework

## Purpose

This framework defines what should be examined when evaluating a potentially habitable exoplanet.

It separates:

**measurement → physical interpretation → chemical modeling → abiotic alternatives → biological interpretation**

That separation is essential.

## 1. Host star

Record:

- Name and catalog identifiers
- Spectral type
- Effective temperature
- Radius
- Mass
- Luminosity
- Metallicity
- Age, when available
- Rotation
- UV activity
- X-ray activity
- Flare behavior
- Stellar variability
- High-energy radiation environment

The stellar spectrum is part of the planetary chemistry problem. Abiotic atmospheric chemistry depends strongly on the incident radiation.

## 2. Planet

Record:

- Planet designation
- Radius
- Mass
- Bulk density
- Surface gravity
- Orbital period
- Semi-major axis
- Eccentricity
- Stellar irradiation
- Equilibrium-temperature estimate
- Transit status
- Transit depth
- Transit duration

A habitable-zone label by itself is insufficient.

## 3. Water and climate

Examine evidence for:

- H2O vapor
- Clouds
- Hazes
- Surface temperature constraints
- Possible liquid-water conditions
- Atmospheric pressure constraints
- Climate stability
- Atmospheric escape
- Possible ocean or surface reservoirs

These are constraints, not automatic evidence of biology.

## 4. Atmospheric inventory

Primary gases:

- H2O
- CO2
- N2
- O2
- O3
- CH4
- CO
- N2O
- NO
- NO2
- HNO3
- NH3
- SO2
- H2S
- OCS, when relevant

For every candidate detection record the measured abundance or constraint, uncertainty, spectral bands used, instrument, and observation date.

## 5. Chemical disequilibrium

Ask:

- Which species coexist?
- What reactions should destroy them?
- What are their modeled atmospheric lifetimes?
- What sources can replenish them?
- What sinks remove them?
- How much free energy separates the observed state from chemical equilibrium?
- Is the disequilibrium atmospheric only, or atmosphere + surface/ocean?

A useful candidate is not merely "gas X detected." It is a **system of mutually constraining species**.

## 6. Abiotic challenge

Every proposed biosignature must be tested against plausible abiotic mechanisms, including:

- CO2 photolysis
- H2O photolysis and hydrogen escape
- volcanic gases
- serpentinization
- hydrothermal chemistry
- lightning chemistry
- impacts
- atmospheric escape
- surface oxidation
- photochemical production and destruction
- stellar UV/XUV effects

A result should be marked as unresolved when a credible abiotic explanation remains viable.

## 7. Persistence

Prefer repeated measurements.

Record:

- Number of transits observed
- Independent observing epochs
- Instruments used
- Consistency between datasets
- Seasonal or temporal variation
- Evidence for changing composition

A repeated signal is more useful than a single marginal detection.

## 8. Independent confirmation

Where possible, seek confirmation using:

- different wavelengths
- different JWST instruments
- independent observatories
- independent retrieval pipelines
- independent stellar/planet models

## Earth comparison

Earth is the reference case.

Relevant terrestrial atmospheric species include O2, O3, CH4, H2O, CO2 and N2O, together with abundant N2.

Earth's atmosphere-ocean system is strongly out of thermodynamic equilibrium because biological activity continually drives chemical composition away from equilibrium.

The study should ask whether an exoplanet displays a similarly difficult-to-explain disequilibrium, rather than searching for one "magic" gas.

## Interpretation rule

The repository should never convert the evidence into a numerical "life score."

Instead report:

- What is measured?
- How certain is it?
- What physical conditions are constrained?
- What abiotic mechanisms have been tested?
- What remains unexplained?
- What observation would distinguish the competing explanations?
