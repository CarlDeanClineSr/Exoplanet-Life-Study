# Earth Calibration Case

## Why Earth is first

Earth is the only known planet with an independently established biosphere.

The purpose of this case is to calibrate the examination method before applying it to unknown worlds.

## The Earth disequilibrium lesson

The important evidence is the coexistence of multiple species maintained away from equilibrium.

High-value examples include:

- abundant O2 alongside reduced gases such as CH4
- N2 + O2 in the presence of liquid water
- trace oxidized and reduced nitrogen species
- continuous exchange among atmosphere, ocean, rock, and biosphere

Earth therefore demonstrates the concept we are trying to recognize remotely:

**a planetary chemical state that is continuously maintained rather than simply established once.**

## Remote-observation translation

NASA explains that an exoplanet transmission spectrum can reveal atmospheric molecules by measuring which wavelengths of starlight are absorbed while the planet crosses its star.

An Earth transmission-spectrum simulation therefore provides a useful teaching reference for:

- H2O
- CO2
- O2
- O3
- CH4
- other atmospheric species

Reference:
https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/science-overview/science-explainers/what-would-earths-atmosphere-look-like-from-the-james-webb-space-telescope/

## Important limitation

Earth's atmosphere is known from direct sampling, ground observations, spacecraft, and decades of atmospheric science.

An exoplanet is not.

Therefore, the same molecular pattern in an exoplanet spectrum is **evidence to be interpreted**, not an automatic identification of life.

## Teaching exercise

Before studying an exoplanet, answer:

1. What would JWST see if it observed Earth as a transiting exoplanet?
2. Which gases would be easiest to detect?
3. Which would be difficult?
4. Which gas combinations would be chemically surprising?
5. Which of those surprises have abiotic explanations?
6. Which additional wavelengths would discriminate among those explanations?
7. How many observations would be needed to establish persistence?
